01 / ABOUT

About me

I’ve always believed that the best customer experiences are the ones where people feel understood, supported, and never left wondering what happens next.

My career began in customer support across SaaS, healthcare, and EdTech. Along the way, I noticed a pattern: customers rarely walk away because of one major failure. More often, it’s the small things that go unresolved: unanswered questions, repeated frustrations, unclear communication, and problems that keep resurfacing. Over time, those moments chip away at trust.

That understanding shaped the way I approach Customer Success. I see it as more than resolving issues or responding when something goes wrong. It’s about understanding what customers need, removing friction before it builds, and making sure they continue to experience value throughout their journey.

At my core, I’m a listener. I pay attention to what customers say, what they struggle to express, and the patterns behind their feedback. I bring empathy to those conversations, but I also believe empathy should lead to action. That means following through, communicating clearly, identifying risks early, and creating practical ways to make the customer experience better.

I’m particularly interested in the connection between customer relationships and business growth: how thoughtful onboarding encourages adoption, how consistent engagement builds trust, and how proactive account management creates opportunities for retention and long-term value.

I believe in anticipating problems before they become disruptions, communicating before customers have to ask, and treating “it’s handled” as a promise rather than a status update. The best problems are often the ones customers never have to experience because someone was paying attention early enough to prevent them.

Today, I’m focused on growing as a Customer Success and Account Management professional, with a particular interest in customer retention, product adoption, account growth, and building relationships that last.

Sunrise Pocket

Customer onboarding and Voice of Customer journey redesign

01
42,600registrations
9,340funded accounts
5,120active after 30 days

The customer problem

The onboarding funnel showed substantial drop-off between registration and 30-day activity. Customers could lose verification progress, struggle to understand account tiers, and receive too little clarity about document status or what to do next. Customer feedback was also spread across care-line calls, app-store reviews, social comments and branch notes, making recurring problems harder to identify and assign.

How I approached it

I mapped the onboarding journey and reviewed the available customer feedback to identify where customers were getting stuck and what they repeatedly needed. The work brought the journey and the Voice of Customer signals together, rather than treating each complaint as an isolated event.

Recommendations and solution design

  • Introduce save-and-resume identity verification, with clearer explanations of BVN/NIN requirements and visible progress.
  • Explain account tiers in plain language, including daily limits, inclusions and a recommended option.
  • Confirm when documents have been received, show their status and explain the next step.
  • Create a structured feedback capture process covering issue category, journey stage, customer comment, experience rating and callback request.
  • Use a clear feedback workflow: Capture, Categorise, Prioritise, Assign, Act and Close the loop.
  • Escalate high-severity themes with context such as source, date, sentiment and reason, so an owner can take action.

Measures to track

The case study sets proposed improvement targets of increasing first-deposit conversion from 47% to 65% and 30-day activation from 55% to 65%. These are proposed targets, not reported achieved results. Other measures include recovered stalled registrations, transfer-related care calls, first-contact resolution and time to first value.

Retention · Customer health

BukkiBooks

Customer health scoring and churn alert system in HubSpot

02
1,000sample accounts
353scored below 50
14 dayskey-feature inactivity trigger

The customer problem

A customer account had stopped using an important inventory-reconciliation feature for five weeks. Overall account activity still appeared healthy, so the change was not noticed promptly. A new store manager had not been trained, and the business had returned to spreadsheets. The case highlighted a gap in how account risk was being detected and followed up.

How I approached it

I designed a weekly health score from 0 to 100 using four signal groups: usage breadth, feature depth, support activity and customer sentiment. The goal was to make changes in customer behaviour visible and give the Customer Success team a consistent way to prioritise outreach.

Solution design

  • Classify accounts as Healthy (80–100), Needs Attention (50–79) or At Risk (below 50).
  • Trigger an alert when a key feature has not been used for 14 days.
  • Flag a silent freeze when weekly activity falls by 30% or more against the account's baseline.
  • Prioritise outreach using risk severity, account value, renewal date and the number of risk signals.
  • Configure a HubSpot proof of concept with health-score segments, an at-risk workflow trigger and an internal email notification linking to the company record.
  • Keep a human conversation at the centre of each alert, because a usage decline signals a change but does not explain its cause.

What the model showed

In the sample dataset, 353 of 1,000 accounts scored below 50. This is a result from the sample-data case study, not a claim about a live customer portfolio. The design separates automated detection from human diagnosis, so the team can focus its attention on accounts where intervention may matter most.

Account recovery · Growth

Kanem Freight

At-risk account recovery and expansion plan

03
45%paid-seat usage before recovery
1,800seats in the recovery scenario
$24,600annualised contract value in scenario

The customer problem

Kanem Freight was considering reducing a 1,200-seat account to 600 seats or cancelling. The departure of a key HR contact, uneven training across locations, declining usage and a lack of regular account reviews had weakened the relationship. In March 2026, 540 of 1,200 paid seats were in use, or 45%, and the number of active administrators had fallen from 14 to 5.

How I approached it

I mapped the account journey from purchase through onboarding, usage decline and renewal risk. I considered the perspectives of the CFO, HR manager, depot staff and drivers to understand why the platform's value was not being experienced consistently across the organisation.

Recovery and growth plan

  • Re-establish the relationship with the CFO, acknowledge the gaps and agree on a 30-day recovery plan.
  • Run focused onboarding for the CFO and HR manager, and restore SMS payslip access.
  • Train the Kano and Port Harcourt teams so adoption is not limited to head office.
  • Re-engage inactive administrators and establish a shared channel for ongoing questions.
  • Introduce structured Voice of Customer tracking, weekly reporting and executive reviews.
  • Use HubSpot and Zapier workflow logic to monitor account health and prompt timely CSM follow-up.

Scenario outcome

The case-study scenario ends with the account expanding from 1,200 to 1,800 seats, 100% seat usage, 18 active administrators and a health score of 80 at Day 90. The annualised contract value in the scenario is $24,600, compared with $16,400 for the original 1,200-seat account. These are scenario figures from the case study, not verified revenue from a live client engagement.

04 / APPROACH

How I think

Start with the customer’s goal

Success looks different for every customer. I begin by understanding what they are trying to achieve.

Notice early signals

Usage, feedback and support history show how an account is doing. I look for patterns before they become problems.

Make onboarding easy

A clear first experience builds the habits that lead to long-term adoption.

Keep the relationship active

Regular, useful contact keeps customers engaged, not only when something goes wrong.

Listen, then act

Customer feedback is only useful when it leads to a clear follow-up.

Work with the team

Good customer outcomes depend on support, product and sales sharing what they learn.
